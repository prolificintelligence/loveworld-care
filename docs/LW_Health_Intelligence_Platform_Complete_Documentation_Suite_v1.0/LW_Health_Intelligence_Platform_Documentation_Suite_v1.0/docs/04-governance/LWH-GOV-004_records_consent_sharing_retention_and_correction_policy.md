# Records, consent, sharing, retention and correction policy

## Record custody and classes

Record classes include identity/grants, institutional applications/evidence/decisions, signed clinical records and addenda, observations/orders/results, medication and procedural events, patient self-reports, care tasks/referrals, finance/claims, editorial content, optional employment/payroll and audit. Each has a custodian, jurisdiction, purpose, access rule, retention schedule and hold/disposal process. Country activation adopts actual durations and response obligations; this suite does not invent one global retention period.

## Patient identity and authority

A patient can exist without an account, including an unidentified emergency patient or newborn. Local identifiers retain issuer/provenance. Cross-facility matching requires reviewed evidence and reversible links; erroneous linkage enters a protected dispute queue. Actor, subject, caregiver, next-of-kin and payer remain distinct. Family relationship alone does not authorise access; age/capacity and representative changes trigger re-evaluation under applicable rules.

## Consent and access grants

Treatment consent/refusal records the proposed intervention, explanation, supported decision/capacity, actual decision, representative/other authority, scope and applicable evidence. Sharing grants independently specify recipient/team, purpose, records/fields, validity, authority and revocation. Marketing/research preferences and app terms are separate. Patients can inspect access history, request correction and manage supported grants. Emergency access, where adopted, is bounded, justified and retrospectively reviewed.

## Integrity, amendments and provenance

Signed records preserve author, professional context, actual event/recorded times, source and version. Addenda/amendments reference the original with reason and new attestation. Entered-in-error marks current-use restrictions while retaining required historical evidence. Patient disputes and self-reports remain attributed; they do not silently rewrite a clinician finding. Corrections invalidate derived summaries/embeddings and notify authorised exchange recipients where relevant. External source documents remain available with checksum and interpretation history.

## Retention and disposal

The adopted retention schedule specifies class, country/operator, trigger, period, child/death/incident considerations, legal hold, archive method and disposal approval. Deletion is controlled across primary stores, replicas, exports and vendor systems to the extent applicable, with backup expiry/restore handling documented. A withdrawal/account closure/affiliation termination is not a command to erase clinical/financial history. Retention does not authorise unnecessary ongoing access or unrelated reuse.

## Disclosure and withdrawal

Release the minimum permitted package after recipient/subject/purpose validation. Record exact source versions and actual delivery/access receipts. Revocation stops future grant-based access and is prioritised to replicas; bounded offline access and delayed revocation are visible limitations. A recipient's already lawfully retained clinical copy follows its custody/retention obligations. Linkable records remain protected even when direct identifiers are masked.

## Custody transfer and exit

Facility closure, network withdrawal, patient move and supplier exit require an accepted custodian, usable human/structured export, source/terminology/attestation/grant history, open-result/referral responsibilities and retention/hold instructions. Verify import and reconciliation before decommissioning. Access recovery and technical restore preserve custody and audit; neither creates new clinical authority. Disputes and complaints retain a reasoned resolution/review trail.
