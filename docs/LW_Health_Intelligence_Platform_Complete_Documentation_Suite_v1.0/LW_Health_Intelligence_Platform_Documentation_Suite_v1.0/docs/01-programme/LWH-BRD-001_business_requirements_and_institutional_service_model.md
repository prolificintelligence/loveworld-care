# Business requirements and institutional service model

## Business service and users

The platform operates a connected health-services network, with institutional participation and direct care as distinct services. Beneficiaries include the public, families, patients, clinicians, facilities, ministry regions/zones and leadership. Participating institutions can be ministry-owned, member-owned or external partners; ownership, affiliation, licensing and capability are separately represented.

## Required business capabilities

1. Register and maintain organisations, facility sites, owners and responsible contacts; resolve duplicates without losing history.
2. Receive applications and evidence; conduct inspections; record central Directorate decisions, conditions, renewal and review.
3. Publish eligible service information and help people find appropriate facilities using capability, geography and current readiness.
4. Define reporting obligations, accept periodic returns and events, verify sources, distinguish missingness and preserve revisions.
5. Produce President, Executive Minister, Directorate, region, zone and facility intelligence with consistent definitions and disclosure controls.
6. Convert findings into interventions, education, support, referral improvement and measured follow-through.
7. Run comprehensive care workflows through the LW EMR or connect existing facility systems; retain locally usable clinical operations during WAN outages.
8. Provide personal/family continuity, clinician assistance, care-linked billing and sponsored access with explicit boundaries.

## Participation service levels

All activated participants have an organisational owner, declared services, evidence obligations and support route. Institutional reporting participation can use direct forms, approved imports or verified interfaces. Native EMR use is an adoption route, not a prerequisite for submitting a required facility return. Clinical service availability depends on verified facility capability and staff privileges. Optional ERP services have separate entitlement and cost schedules.

## End-to-end operational obligations

Every submitted application, return, event, referral and patient request receives a durable acknowledgement and responsible queue. Received, reviewed, accepted, fulfilled and closed are separate claims. Work remains owned until completed or explicitly accepted by a successor. A missed deadline creates an owned escalation; a delivered notification does not satisfy a clinical or reporting obligation. Users see status, next action and expected update under approved service commitments.

## Information and commercial boundaries

Leadership views are aggregate-first with source, coverage, period and freshness. Coded referrals are purpose-limited clinical exchanges. Identified records require valid authority and correct-patient matching. Sponsors receive entitlement/financial information and permitted impact measures. Institutional roles do not automatically grant clinical access. Core platform funding is Directorate-led; care and optional service revenue are separately measured. Care urgency remains clinically determined, independent of account balance.

## Business acceptance

Acceptance requires a complete institutional loop and complete care journeys across all confirmed facility types, including unavailable service, partial information, declined referral, corrections, staff absence, outages and reconciliation. Facility and coordinator users must complete their tasks without recreating already available data. The Director sees both progress and outstanding gaps. The project records actual reporting burden, patient task success, clinician workload, unresolved care and operating costs so leadership can decide where to improve or expand.

## Scope boundaries and extensions

The core includes patient billing, pharmacy stock and diagnostic workflows associated with care. Full accounting, procurement, HR and payroll are optional modules. Disease-specific automated treatment protocols, unfamiliar country practice and additional languages require their own validated packages. These are controlled activation boundaries, not permission to invent missing medical rules or promise universally available services.
