# Programme charter and executive outcome framework

## Mission and programme result

LW Health Intelligence Platform supplies the operating and intelligence foundation for the ministry's worldwide health-services network. It connects institutions, clinical teams, regional/zonal coordination, patients and approved partners through consistent records, standards, reporting and accountable action. Its public identity remains recognisably LoveWorld while access to personal care does not require church membership.

## Outcome hierarchy

| Outcome | Evidence of useful progress | Accountable role |
|---|---|---|
| Know the network | Verified institutions, ownership, capabilities, contacts and current recognition | Network lead |
| Improve standards | Reviewed evidence, inspections, corrective action, renewal and fair review | Standards lead |
| Understand health/service conditions | Defined returns and events with visible completeness, verification and limitations | Health information lead |
| Act responsibly | Leadership decisions with owners, resources, due dates and outcome reviews | Directorate operations |
| Deliver attentive care | Appropriate access, clinical ownership, safe handover and follow-up | Clinical operator |
| Sustain the system | Funded core operations and measured care/service contribution | Directorate finance |

Visits and report counts are activity, not proof of better population health. The programme evaluates access, continuity, safety, reporting reliability, standards improvement, operating burden and sustainable resources together. Baselines are measured during pilot preparation. No revenue or health-impact target is claimed as already achieved.

## Programme composition

Institutional services comprise registry/directory, affiliation/inspection/renewal, periodic/event reporting, validation, role-specific intelligence, intervention management, education and community/referral coordination. LW Care comprises public patient/family experience, clinician workspaces, comprehensive facility EMR, record exchange and care-linked financial workflows. Shared services provide identity, hierarchy, rules, data, audit, local runtime, synchronisation and governed intelligence. Optional enterprise modules extend accounting, procurement, HR and payroll through controlled interfaces.

## Investment and responsibility

The leading proposal is Directorate funding for core platform development and operation, with care and optional services generating revenue where approved. The charter does not establish a budget, signed purchase order or price. Core registry and standards decisions remain independent of commercial treatment. Prolific Intelligence's technical delivery and managed-service responsibilities are contractually defined in LWH-COM-002; ministry product ownership, source licences and component intellectual property require explicit agreement rather than inferred transfer.

## Delivery and success gates

The initial demonstration proves registration → evidence/inspection → Directorate decision → directory → facility/zone return → verified intelligence → assigned intervention. The integrated clinical demonstration additionally proves outpatient, inpatient, maternal/child and diagnostic workflows, medication handling, referral/record sharing and WAN-offline operation with subsequent reconciliation. The full confirmed clinical scope remains in the implementation baseline even when delivery increments are sequenced.

Countries and named pilot facilities remain unselected. Global architecture and reusable country packages are designed now. Live activation requires an adopted country package, actual facility/staff readiness, consent/records governance, tested local continuity and funded support. Programme reviews consider patient and clinician experience, reporting burden, care obligations, errors and hidden unmet demand alongside technical metrics.
